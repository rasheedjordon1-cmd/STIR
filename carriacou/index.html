Product arrives inside the DC's window. Documented. No chargeback.

Any warehouse can keep a box cold. The money is lost at the receiving door: a missed appointment, a short ASN, a lot code that doesn't match the paperwork. This operation is built around that door.

Delivery appointments
Every outbound load is scheduled against the DC's receiving window before it leaves the dock. The appointment number rides on the bill of lading.
OTIF
Loads are built and dispatched to land on time and in full. A late load and a short load cost you the same chargeback, so both are treated as failures here, not at your retailer's deduction desk.
ASN and EDI
Advance ship notices and EDI transmission: TK. Whether notices originate from this warehouse or from your team is being confirmed and will be stated here plainly.
Lot codes and rotation
Every pallet is received against a lot code and rotated FIFO or FEFO, whichever your retailer's routing guide calls for.
Temperature record
Zone temperatures are logged by TK. The record is available for any load on request.
Recall traceability
Lot-level trace from receipt to the DC door. If a lot is pulled, you know which pallets, which loads, and which docks.
Lumper coordination
Unloading and lumper fees at the DC are coordinated ahead of the appointment, so the driver isn't negotiating at the door.
Routing guide compliance
Retail routing guide handling: TK.

Stored and delivered by one operation.

One accountable party from your receipt to the retailer's dock. No handoff between a warehouse and a carrier who point at each other.

  1. 1

    Receive

    Your product comes in on appointment, is checked against the lot code and the temperature on arrival, and is put away in its zone.

  2. 2

    Store

    Pallet positions are billed by the month. Rotation and the temperature record run the whole time it sits.

  3. 3

    Deliver

    On request, a refrigerated Sprinter van or truck moves it to your customer's dock inside their receiving window.
